3i
Teaching simplifications — declared, not hidden
Four rotor poles and twelve stator slots are drawn for legibility. Real machines use many more.
The three phase currents are drawn as one dot or cross per coil group. Real windings are distributed across many slots.
The resultant field is drawn as a single arrow. A real air-gap field is a distribution, not a vector at one point.
Field strength is shown by arrow length and coil brightness only. No magnitude is claimed.
The load angle is exaggerated so the gap is visible at a glance.
Flux paths are drawn as a few clean arcs. Real flux fills the whole steel section.
The cutaway shows one magnet layer per pole. Two and three-layer rotors are also built.
Magnet and reluctance torque are shown as two arrows on one shaft. They are not simply added in this proportion at every speed.
Rotation is drawn at a speed the eye can follow, unrelated to any real rpm.
Nothing in this chapter asserts a dimension, a material grade, a supplier or a market position.
Every simplification above is reversible in the build: the layer ids allow a higher pole count, a distributed winding or a multi-layer rotor to be swapped in without changing the interface, the labels or the motion sheet.
