Grains, heat and dysprosium
Why does a magnet weaken when it gets hot?
A sintered magnet is a mosaic of microscopic grains, each with its own magnetisation. Heat lets grains at weak boundaries flip.

Mechanical assumptions in these three scenes
Vehicle is a generic rear-drive layout; body outline is schematic, not a model of any car.
Battery drawn as a floor-mounted pack between the axles; cell count and module split are illustrative.
Motor shown as a single rear drive unit with a coaxial single-speed reducer.
Stator drawn with 48 slots and rotor with 8 poles as a common teaching ratio, not a specification.
Magnets shown in a single-layer V arrangement; layer count is a design choice, not a constant.
Two bearings, one each side of the rotor. No cooling jacket, resolver or terminal box drawn yet.
Grain mosaic is schematic: grain size, shape and boundary thickness are not to scale.
No dimensions, torque, power, efficiency or company claims are asserted anywhere in this shell.
