The question

Can an electric car keep the performance of a magnet motor without the magnets?

To answer that, you have to see what the magnets are actually doing. So we start with the whole car, and we don't stop until we are holding a single block of neodymium.

Scroll 7 stops
01 · The car

Two motors move this car.

One at each axle. Everything else in the floor — battery, inverter, cooling — exists to feed them. The motors are the only parts that turn electricity into movement.

02 · The drive unit

Pull one out. It fits in a cabin bag.

A drive unit is a motor bolted to a single-speed reduction gearbox. Roughly 300 mm across, 40 kg, and good for 150–250 kW — more power per kilogram than any engine that came before it.

03 · Teardown

Five parts, and only one of them moves.

The stator is a stack of steel teeth with copper wound through every slot. It stays still. Inside it, separated by about a millimetre of air, sits the rotor. That's the whole machine.

04 · The field

Nothing spins the copper. The copper spins a field.

Three sets of coils are fed currents timed a third of a cycle apart. Their fields add up to one magnetic direction that sweeps around the bore. The rotor's magnets lock onto it and are dragged along — permanently in step, always a few degrees behind.

05 · The material

This is the part that ties the car to a map.

Sixteen sintered blocks. Buried in pairs, tilted into a V so the steel bridges hold them against 16,000 rpm and shield them from the stator's own field. Together they weigh one to two kilograms.

06 · Rare-earth-free

Keep the stator. Change what answers it.

The rotating field stays exactly the same. Every alternative is a different way of giving that field something to pull on — a wound coil, an induced current, or just the shape of the steel.

The answer

Yes — the field can come from somewhere else. The purpose of the machine never changes; the tradeoffs move into copper, steel, heat and control.

That is the whole argument for a rare-earth-free drivetrain, and it is why the engineering fight has shifted from mining to geometry and software.
